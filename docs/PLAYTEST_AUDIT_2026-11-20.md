# Games of Hunger playtest and implementation plan

Engine 1.30.0, content 1.37.0, commit `7f69b53`. The product code was left as tested. The full project check passed, including 423 Vitest tests and 69 browser tests.

## Can hatred build across seasons?

**A returning player can remember an old enemy in the story. Their mechanical grudge starts fresh from the cast setup.** A player can hold several grudges at once, and each direction is independent. A can hate B while B feels neutral about A. Within a game, the engine calls the living target with the highest outgoing grudge of at least 40 the player's nemesis. Player ID breaks ties. The roster calls that target **Rival**. The relationship network labels individual edges **Rival** at 40–79 and **Nemesis** at 80–100, so a player can have several Nemesis edges but only one active target for a scene condition.

Any player can gain hostility through events such as theft or injury. Theft costs the victim 35 trust toward the thief and adds 45 grudge. With personality enabled, the **Grudge** trait adds a special reaction when a credited killer eliminates a mutual ally. The affected player's grudge toward that killer reaches 100 and stays at 100 through subsequent adjustments in that season. Some revenge outcomes require an earlier qualifying fact from the current game. A manually set starting relationship follows the cast into new seasons. **Love and Hate** can set extreme opinions at the start based on the seed and any configured opinion.

New games use the configured cast relationships. With **Include past season events** enabled, completed seasons contribute up to 12 candidate memories matched by stable player ID. Renaming works with the same ID. The engine uses up to four spaced callbacks when the current scene fits. A nemesis callback generally needs both returning players to be bound in a qualifying conflict. It does not change trust, grudge, encounter eligibility, revenge evidence, or outcomes.

In six five-season histories, all 30 paired lore-on/lore-off games matched mechanically, including interviews, placements and awards. The lore-enabled games produced 48 callbacks, including 27 about pairs and six about nemeses. Two of 24 returning seasons had zero callbacks. In a targeted example, contestant 13 reached grudge 100 toward contestant 05 after a credited ally death, then opened the next season at grudge 0 toward that same ID. ## What the demos showed

The neutral 24-player batches used the same 200 seeds at each danger setting. The shipped Campfire example is a separate four-player cast with traits and a starting alliance. Opening deaths, travel and fights below are per game. The finale percentage uses all eliminations as its denominator.

| Cast and danger | Games | Deciding day | Opening deaths | Day/night fights | Voluntary travel passages | Finale deaths |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 24 neutral, low | 200 | 5.93 | 5.40 | 28.90 | 3.48 | 23.9% |
| 24 neutral, normal | 200 | 5.57 | 5.40 | 27.49 | 2.35 | 16.5% |
| 24 neutral, high | 200 | 4.43 | 5.40 | 23.74 | 0.57 | 11.1% |
| 4, Campfire, normal | 100 | 2.12 | 1.01 | 1.90 | 0.06 | 37.7% |

- **Finales struggle at both cast-size extremes.** The four-player cast used the boundary fallback in 13 of 100 games. All 30 mutual Loyal duos ended through that same fallback, and all 60 neutral duos ended in the opening. All 40 normal-danger 48-player games reached Day 6 with six to 13 players still alive when the finale began. The finale then accounted for 325 of 1,880 eliminations in that cohort. [Finale routing and fallback](../src/engine/index.ts)
- **Common passages dominate the arena.** The leading normalized fight passage appeared in 111 of 200 normal games. The leading passage reached 117 of 200 on low and 118 of 200 on high. In the normal batch, the elbow-counter passage came from four template IDs. The two season recaps read from the browser also exposed item-label grammar such as “collects emergency blanket. oil lantern and signal mirror remain”. [Analytics normalization](../src/analytics/demo.ts) · [Resupply text](../src/content/resupply.ts)
- **Danger has a narrower effect than its label suggests.** Bloodbath death counts matched per seed across all 200 triples. The opening has a separate casualty budget. High danger leaves substantially less room for ordinary travel, so it also changes how often users see the six-region arena. [Opening budget](../src/engine/index.ts#L150)
- **Nemesis records sometimes describe a ranking change as a new rivalry.** In 70 deliberately rivalry-rich 24-player games, 164 of 1,037 new active-nemesis facts selected an edge that was already at grudge 40 or higher. For 142 of those, at least one member was absent from the triggering scene. At event 17 of `nov20-special-rivals-0`, contestant 11 eliminated contestant 12 and inherited contestant 09 as their active rival. The archive said 11 “became a nemesis of” 09, which reads as the opposite direction. The staged cast produced 220 ally-death grudges, but only 10 games showed the dedicated staredown and one showed the dedicated Grudge confrontation. Other actions can still express grudges or pay them off. [Fact emission](../src/engine/mechanics.ts#L167) · [Archive copy](../src/lore/index.ts#L17)
- **Demo Lab supports aggregation better than comparison.** In the browser, it rejected a second run with the same seeds after the danger setting changed. New seeds worked, but both cohorts fed the same averages. The current-cast option accepted a draft with an unnamed character, started the worker and printed raw validation JSON. A standalone 1,000-game neutral batch completed and exported, then restored all 1,000 games after reload. It took 3 minutes 24 seconds on this machine and stored 3.67 million characters in one localStorage entry. The worker sends the cumulative stats object after every game. [Demo controls](../src/ui/DemoLab.tsx) · [Demo worker](../src/analytics/demo.worker.ts)
- **Returning seasons duplicate named casts.** After saving the sample cast, finishing a game, and clicking **Start another season**, starting that season produced a second saved slot with the same title and lineup. The continuation clears the slot ID and the start action automatically saves. Both season replays remained available, and the cast's player IDs were reused. The same walkthrough verified phase playback, recap downloads, and the archive at 390px. [Start and autosave](../src/ui/App.tsx#L266) · [Season continuation](../src/ui/App.tsx#L590)
- **Seed help leaves out the history input.** The game settings promise “The same cast, settings, and seed produce the same game.” In a two-player browser test, replaying that setup after completing its first season produced the same winner and event, added a past-season callback to the recap, and created a second archive entry. The canonical ID intentionally hashes the full input, including the history snapshot. Explain that dependency in the seed help and point users to the saved replay for an exact retelling. ## Five improvements and how to build them

| Priority | Improvement | Implementation and verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Campaign grudges** | Add an opt-in relationship carryover setting alongside story callbacks. Derive a directional ledger from completed seasons using stable player IDs and source run/event references. Start with a bounded score for betrayal, theft and witnessed ally deaths. Give older incidents less weight and allow recorded reconciliation to reduce the score. Dedupe source events and cap the opening carryover so repeated seasons cannot lock the whole cast into hostility. Snapshot the resolved values and evidence in the new replay input, then blend with configured relationships under a documented precedence rule for Love and Hate and manual presets. Start with encounter eligibility and weighting. Add cross-season revenge only after replay validation can verify its source. Test both directions, renamed and removed players, disabled carryover, imported or deleted seasons, and same-seed repeatability across five-season campaigns. |
| 2 | **Rivalry timeline and clearer season continuation** | Use one shared active-rival selector and label the network's edge strength separately. Record a newly earned grudge, a switch to an existing rival, and a resolved or unavailable rival as different cases. Show only revealed changes during a game and link completed changes to source events in the archive. Use directionally explicit copy such as “A now considers B their main rival.” Keep old replay and backup derivation compatible. Preserve the named cast slot when continuing the current saved cast. If continuing a historical snapshot with no matching slot, require an explicit save action before creating another slot. Test a rival dying, one-way hostility, a seeded starting rival, and consecutive seasons from both current and archived casts. |
| 3 | **Cast-size-aware pacing and finales** | Extract the opening budget and finale transition into a size-aware policy. Give two-player games a setup beat before a deciding scene. Add a neutral scored contest or arena challenge for mutual Loyal finalists, respecting the existing prohibition on attacking an ally. Increase pre-finale attrition for large casts through staged narrowing, then keep the Day 6 finish guarantee. Publish where danger takes effect in settings. Compare matched 2-, 4-, 8-, 24- and 48-player cohorts across danger levels. Track opening finish rate, fallback rate, finalist count and deciding day. Verify supported custom packs still reach a winner. |
| 4 | **Paired demo experiments** | Store results by versioned experiment and variant with a fingerprint of cast, content and settings. Allow shared seeds across distinct variants, and dedupe identical inputs. Validate the cast and settings before starting the worker with the same user-readable errors as the game setup. Report per-arm averages, paired differences and downloadable seeds. Send small progress messages and periodic checkpoints, and use IndexedDB for run summaries. Reuse Trait Impact's checkpoint patterns where they fit. Test paired normal/high runs, partial completion, reload, export, and the maximum supported batch. |
| 5 | **Variety budget for common story beats** | Use shared identifiers for recurring scene situations across authoring variants and template IDs. Extend the demo leaderboard with situation frequency, representative text and the seed to replay it. Start by expanding the two dominant counterattack situations and reducing their recurrence within a phase and game. Fix item phrases in the shared resupply formatter at the same time. Compare matched seeds and review sample recaps. Track top-situation frequency, within-game repeats and combat-method shares, and distinguish content that was ineligible from content that was eligible but rarely selected. |

Start with the rivalry facts and demo comparison work. Both provide evidence for tuning campaign carryover, and the pacing and variety proposals can then reuse the same paired experiments.

## Reproduce the main batches

Use a scratch directory for outputs. The CLI appends `-0` through `-199` to the base seed for the danger comparison.

```text
node scripts/demo-runs.mjs --count 200 --seed nov20-audit --danger low --output low.json
node scripts/demo-runs.mjs --count 200 --seed nov20-audit --danger normal --output normal.json
node scripts/demo-runs.mjs --count 200 --seed nov20-audit --danger high --output high.json
node scripts/demo-runs.mjs --count 100 --seed nov20-audit-small --cast public/examples/cast.json --output small.json
node scripts/past-season-audit.mjs past-season-results
```

The additional 200 engine games used normal danger and default settings. Player IDs ran from `audit-01` up to the cast size in each case. The seed format was `nov20-special-${cohort}-${index}`, starting at index 0. Cohorts were 60 neutral duos (`duo`), 30 mutually Loyal duos at reciprocal trust 60 (`loyalDuo`), 40 neutral 48-player casts (`large`), and 70 rivalry-rich 24-player casts built from eight groups of three (`rivals`). Each rivalry group gave the first player Grudge, the second Disloyal, reciprocal trust 70 between those two, and starting trust −55 and grudge 60 from the second to the third. Those are deliberate stress cases. The phases, layouts and storage flows were tested in Chromium via Microsoft Edge on Windows.
